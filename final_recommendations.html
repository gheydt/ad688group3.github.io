Final Recommendations

This section provides career recommendations for students and entry-level applicants pursuing Data Engineering opportunities in the Insurance Carrier industry (NAICS 5241). The recommendations focus on suitable career pathways, employment locations, potential employers, and technical skill development.

The findings indicate that Data Engineering offers attractive earning potential, but experience requirements and technical skill gaps can make direct entry challenging. Therefore, the recommendations emphasize practical ways to build qualifications, identify relevant opportunities, and prepare for long-term career growth.

The recommendations build on the Career Evaluation, Market Baseline, and Skill Gap Analysis findings.

Career Pathways

Based on the Career Evaluation and Skill Gap Analysis findings, entering Data Engineering directly may be challenging for students and entry-level applicants. The analyzed positions had a median minimum experience requirement of five years, while several important technical skills require further development. Therefore, applicants should consider related entry-level positions that can provide practical experience before transitioning into Data Engineering.

Data Analyst is a recommended starting point for applicants with existing SQL knowledge. This role can help develop experience in querying databases, cleaning data, preparing reports, and working with business information. It provides a foundation for understanding how organizations use data to support decisions.

Junior Data Engineer is a more direct career pathway for applicants who have developed foundational Python, SQL, and ETL skills. Entry-level engineering responsibilities can help build experience with data pipelines, databases, and cloud technologies, preparing candidates for more advanced Data Engineering positions.

Business Intelligence (BI) Analyst is another potential entry-level pathway. This role provides opportunities to strengthen SQL, data modeling, reporting, and data visualization skills while gaining experience working with business stakeholders.

Overall, Data Analyst and BI Analyst positions are practical starting points, while Junior Data Engineer is the most direct pathway toward a long-term Data Engineering career. Applicants should prioritize roles that offer opportunities to develop Python, SQL, cloud computing, and data pipeline skills.

Locations

Location is an important consideration for applicants pursuing Data Engineering careers in the Insurance Carrier industry (NAICS 5241). The Market Baseline identified relevant job postings across 10 states, indicating that opportunities are geographically distributed rather than limited to a single location.

For entry-level applicants, the recommended approach is to prioritize locations with the highest concentration of relevant job postings, while also considering access to internships, related data positions, and professional development opportunities. Areas with multiple potential employers may provide greater flexibility when searching for an initial position or transitioning into Data Engineering.

Remote work should also be considered when evaluating opportunities. Nine of the 29 analyzed postings explicitly mentioned remote work, while 20 did not specify a work arrangement. Applicants should therefore explore remote opportunities but verify each position’s location and work requirements before applying.

Overall, applicants should focus on locations where relevant opportunities are available while remaining flexible about relocation, hybrid arrangements, and related entry-level roles. A broader geographic search may increase access to potential career pathways.

Employers

The Market Baseline identified seven employers advertising Data Engineering and related positions within the Insurance Carrier industry (NAICS 5241). These findings suggest that applicants should consider both the number of available opportunities and the experience requirements when selecting potential employers.

State Farm is an important employer to consider because it accounted for approximately 52% of the 29 analyzed job postings. This indicates a strong presence in the observed job market, although repeated advertisements do not necessarily represent separate vacancies. Applicants should review State Farm’s opportunities while paying particular attention to required experience, technical skills, and entry-level eligibility.

Rather than focusing exclusively on one employer, applicants should also explore the other insurance companies represented in the dataset. A diversified employer search may provide access to different technologies, work arrangements, and career-development opportunities. Applicants should prioritize organizations offering internships, junior-level positions, or related data roles that provide opportunities to develop practical Data Engineering skills.

Overall, State Farm is a priority employer to investigate based on posting volume, but applicants should compare multiple organizations and focus on positions that align with their current qualifications and long-term career goals.

Skill Development Priorities

The Skill Gap Analysis identified Cloud Computing, Python, Git & CI/CD, and Spark / PySpark as the highest-priority areas for technical development. These skills appeared frequently in the analyzed Data Engineering job postings, while the team’s self-reported proficiency remained relatively low. Addressing these gaps would help improve readiness for Data Engineering careers in the Insurance Carrier industry.

Python and Cloud Computing should be primary development priorities because they are important for building data pipelines, automating processing tasks, and managing cloud-based data infrastructure. Applicants should gain practical experience through programming exercises, cloud training, and projects involving data storage and transformation.

Git & CI/CD and Spark / PySpark should also receive significant attention. Git supports collaboration and version control, while CI/CD practices help automate development workflows. Spark and PySpark provide experience processing larger datasets, making them valuable skills for more advanced Data Engineering responsibilities.

Applicants should also strengthen ETL/ELT and data pipeline development, while building on their existing SQL knowledge. Combining these skills in practical projects can demonstrate the ability to collect, transform, validate, and manage data.

Overall, the recommended approach is to prioritize the largest skill gaps, develop hands-on projects, and build a portfolio demonstrating technical proficiency. This provides a structured path toward stronger qualifications and greater career readiness.

Limitations

The career and skill-development recommendations are based on 29 Data Engineering-related job postings from the Insurance Carrier industry (NAICS 5241). Because the sample represents a limited number of employers and positions, the findings may not reflect the entire insurance job market or current hiring conditions.

The Skill Gap Analysis uses self-reported proficiency ratings from three team members on a scale of 1 to 5. These ratings have not been independently validated through technical assessments or professional evaluations. Additionally, the market demand scores are derived from how frequently skills appear in job postings, rather than the proficiency levels employers explicitly require. Therefore, the calculated skill gaps should be interpreted as development indicators, not direct measures of job qualifications.

Insurance Knowledge was included in the team’s self-assessment but excluded from the quantitative Skill Gap Matrix because a comparable market demand score was not available.

The recommended career pathways, locations, and employers are based on the available market evidence and should not be treated as guarantees of employment. Applicants should review current job requirements, verify work arrangements, and consider their individual qualifications when making career decisions.
