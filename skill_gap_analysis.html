Skill Gap Analysis

This analysis evaluates how well the team’s current skills align with the skills most frequently requested for Data Engineer-related roles in the Insurance industry.

The analysis began with 1,908 Insurance industry job postings. To focus on our target career, the dataset was filtered to Data Engineer and closely related positions, including Data Engineering, Analytics Engineer, Data Platform Engineer, ETL Engineer, and Data Warehouse Engineer roles. This resulted in 29 Data Engineer-related job postings used for the market skill analysis.

The analysis focused on the following Data Engineering skills:

Python, SQL, ETL/Pipelines, Cloud Computing, Spark/PySpark, Databricks, Data Modeling, Databases/Snowflake, Git/CI-CD, and Data Quality/Governance.

Scoring Method

Each team member rated their current proficiency using a 1–5 scale:

  • 1 = Beginner
  • 2 = Basic Knowledge
  • 3 = Intermediate
  • 4 = Advanced
  • 5 = Expert

The individual ratings were averaged to calculate the Team Average for each skill.

Market demand was calculated by measuring the percentage of the 29 relevant job postings that mentioned each skill. The percentages were then converted to a 1–5 market-demand scale:

  • 1 = Less than 20% of postings
  • 2 = 20%–39.9%
  • 3 = 40%–59.9%
  • 4 = 60%–79.9%
  • 5 = 80%–100%

The Market Score measures how frequently employers request a skill; it does not represent the proficiency level employers expect.

The skill gap was calculated as:

Skill Gap = Market Score − Team Average

If the Team Average was greater than the Market Score, the gap was recorded as zero.

Development priority was classified as:

  • High Priority: Skill Gap ≥ 2
  • Moderate Priority: Skill Gap ≥ 1 and < 2
  • Low Priority: Skill Gap < 1

A larger gap therefore represents a skill that appears frequently in the analyzed job postings but where the team’s current proficiency is comparatively lower.

Key Findings

The Skill Gap Analysis identified several important areas for technical skill development among the team. Cloud Computing and Python showed the largest measured skill gaps (3.67 each), followed by Git/CI-CD and Spark/PySpark (3.00 each). These skills appeared frequently in the analyzed job postings, while the team’s current proficiency ratings were relatively low, making them the highest priorities for further development.

ETL/Pipelines showed a moderate skill gap of 1.67, with market demand of 62% and a team average proficiency of 2.33. In comparison, SQL was one of the team’s stronger technical skills, with a team average of 3.33 and a relatively small measured gap of 0.67. Data Quality, Data Modeling, DB/Snowflake, and Databricks showed no measured gaps under the current scoring method. However, a zero gap does not necessarily indicate that the team has mastered these skills or meets every employer’s expectations.

Insurance Knowledge was included in the team’s self-assessment but excluded from the quantitative skill-gap comparison because the structured job-posting data did not provide a directly comparable measure of industry-specific knowledge.

The analysis was based on 29 Data Engineer-related job postings in the Insurance Carrier industry (NAICS 5241), published between May and September 2024. Market demand was converted into a 1–5 score based on how frequently each skill appeared in the postings, rather than the proficiency level required by employers. Therefore, the calculated skill gaps should be interpreted as indicators of development priorities rather than direct measurements of employer-required proficiency. These findings reflect the analyzed sample and should not be generalized to the entire insurance industry.
